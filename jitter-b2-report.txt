Question 1 — Does the app-owned HardwareRenderer block more?

In this alternating AVD sample, yes: HEAD spent much more time in long posts and finished late more often. It was not just a longer wait caused by starting the post earlier while preserving the same completion time. HEAD-1 flat and HEAD-3 at 40 degrees had sustained roughly one-refresh posting delays: postNanos medians 15,268,667 and 15,818,937.5 ns; done-minus-vsync medians 19,901,500 and 20,094,787 ns. Their median posting start was 4,192,667 and 4,213,981.5 ns after vsync, later than the corresponding variants' 2,079,891 and 2,101,527 ns. The variant also had a shorter sustained-delay episode in its first flat run.

After the first 5 s, HEAD had 4,256 posts >8 ms and 2,013 >16 ms; VARIANT had 593 and 291. Across the test's own summary windows, HEAD had 4,439 overruns / 4,438 late frames versus VARIANT's 775 / 775. Pooled median completion was 3,883,870 ns for HEAD versus 3,548,379 ns for VARIANT. This difference is dominated by intermittent sustained-delay episodes, not a uniform fixed overhead: in most later steady scenarios the posting medians were similar. HEAD's 40-degree maximum work was 11.559, 9.308, 25.807, 11.238 ms; the round-1 pattern of a 20+ ms frame in most HEAD runs did not reproduce here.

All scenario fps values still round to 60.0, and missed-vsync counters total only 4 for HEAD and 2 for VARIANT. Therefore the evidence shows longer synchronous posting and later completion in this sample, without establishing a corresponding loss of displayed frames. The host/GLES environment and run-to-run variation limit causal attribution beyond this comparison. It does not establish phone behavior or explain why the renderer entered the delayed phase.

Metric caveat: VARIANT recordNanos starts after lockHardwareCanvas, whereas HEAD's includes target.begin and recording. VARIANT syncResult is a constant zero; HEAD records HardwareRenderer's result. Question 2 — What is the long stall?

All six traces identify the approximately 200 ms stall as an allocation waiting for ART background concurrent mark-compact GC to complete. In every trace the worker has a GC: Wait For Completion Alloc slice, a roughly 196–199 ms Sleeping interval, and a HeapTaskDaemon wakeup at GC completion. The collector is almost continuously on CPU during that collection, with substantial root/mark-stack scanning, reference processing, and compaction. This is not first-use JIT or a long JNA monitor wait in these captures. It occurs in all three checks runs too.

Short JNA Cleaner monitor contention does exist, with Cleaner.register waiting against Cleaner.remove / the cleaner monitor. The longest such worker slice per run is listed below; all are sub-millisecond. No contention/monitor slice on the worker is >=5 ms in any of these six traces. Thus the suggested JNA-cleanup monitor cause is not confirmed for the long stall. The checks traces have additional 10–13 ms FullSuspendCheck waits overlapping GC marking pauses; two are awakened by JNA Cleaner, but the overlapping ART slices identify GC suspension, and a wakeup by JNA Cleaner does not identify it as a lock owner.

The trace does not provide a Java lock object/owner for GC: Wait For Completion Alloc, and blocked_function is NULL in all selected state rows. There is no observed long monitor lock to name. The relevant GC thread, wakeup, and exact overlapping slices are reported below. The short monitor slices do name JNA Cleaner and Cleaner.register/remove explicitly.

These captures name the AVD tight-loop stall. They do not prove that the phone's separately observed 60–93 ms gameplay stalls have the same cause, nor identify which allocation sites account for the GC pressure. No phone was touched.
